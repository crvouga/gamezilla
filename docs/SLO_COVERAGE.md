# SLO coverage

What the service level objectives cover, what they are measured from, how
quickly a failure pages, and what they still cannot see. Measured
2026-10-26 against Grafana Cloud stack `shortawning2091` (read-only queries).
Every objective is defined in OpenSLO under `specs/<context>/` (product
words, `# live`); its measurement, Grafana SLO, alert rules and routing are
generated by
[`libs/observability-shared-ts`](../libs/observability-shared-ts/README.md#service-level-objectives);
each has a runbook section in [`SLO_RUNBOOK.md`](SLO_RUNBOOK.md).

### Why these targets and windows

- **7-day rolling windows**: the backend keeps about 14 days, and an alert's
  longest window (3 days) must fit inside the objective's.
- **Tier 0 at 99.95%** where the evidence resolves it: minute-by-minute
  checks (10,080 minutes a week) and people's requests. The broker check
  (1,008 a week) resolves 99.5%. The Health workflow samples at most hourly
  (35 to 170 runs a week), so its objectives are 97%, 95% and 90% by tier;
  anything stricter is breached by one flaky run.
- **Budgeting**: people's requests, saves and background rounds by
  Occurrences; every check from outside by **Timeslices** (1 min, 10 min,
  1 h): a slice is good only when every check in it passed, so one bad hour
  of one game counts as a bad hour. Grafana SLO has no time slices; its
  dashboards show the share of good samples, and the hourly
  `health:objectives` job is the judge.
- **Latency** at today's baselines (agreed), not at "95% < 100 ms / 99.9% <
  500 ms", which today's sync path (p90 750 ms) cannot meet.

### Alerting

| Cadence | Page (critical) | Ticket (warning) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| standard (people's requests, work, minute checks) | 14.4x over 1 h and 5 min; 6x over 6 h and 30 min | 3x over 1 d and 2 h; 1x over 3 d and 6 h |
| ten-minutely (broker) | 14.4x over 3 h and 30 min | 1x over 3 d and 6 h |
| sparse (Health, about hourly) | 10x over 12 h and 4 h | 1x over 3 d and 12 h |

- Every rule of a sliced indicator burns **per slice** and raises one alert
  per failing slice, named in its `slice` annotation. Before this, one game
  failing every run was 1 bad sample in 13, a 2.6x burn that never paged.
- A burn needs enough events in its long window (60 an hour for people's
  requests). Below that, **low-traffic counts** take over: one failure a
  person meets pushes, three in an hour page.
- **Silence**: each source of evidence behind a tier 0 or 1 objective has a
  rule (no Health result for 8 h, no minute check for 10 min, no broker check
  for 40 min, no background round for 15 min: page; no people's traffic for
  a day: push).
- **Certificates** for the web, API and broker hosts: push at 14 days, page
  at 3 days.
- No tier asks for more than three quarters of the fastest burn its target
  allows; the minute checks hold their fast burn 6 minutes so one flaky
  probe does not page.
- Routing (`route` label): tier 0 and 1 critical alerts in production page
  through Grafana IRM; every other critical alert, and every tier 0 or 1
  warning, pushes; tier 2 warnings go to the daily digest. Grafana SLO's own
  alerting is not used: its fixed windows cannot follow a check's cadence or
  split by slice.

## Fault-injection plan

Run each in **dev** (shares the stack; its `page` alerts push, never page)
unless stated. Times include the rule interval (1 min fast, 5 min slow), the
metric export interval (about 1 min), the rule's hold and IRM delivery.

| Objective | Fault | Expected alert | Expected time to notify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `the-app-is-always-reachable` | Make dev's readiness fail (point dev's `DATABASE_URL` at a closed port in a dev-only deploy) with dev's `every_seconds` at 60 | `fast-burn (page)` with `slice` `api-ready-dev` | about 8 min (1-min probe, 6-min hold) |
| `the-app-opens-quickly`, `the-app-never-hangs-on-opening` | A dev build that sleeps 1.5 s, then 3 s, in `/api/health/ready` | `the-app-opens-quickly: fast-burn`, then `the-app-never-hangs-on-opening: fast-burn`, `slice` `api-ready-dev` | about 8 min |
| `live-updates-can-connect` | Point dev's broker check at a host without the broker (`broker-missing.<dev zone>`) | `fast-burn (page)` | about 25 min (two failed ten-minute checks) |
| any Health objective | One feature broken on dev only (for example the Google mock answering 500 through its `/__admin` faults), Health dispatched hourly (`bun health`) | that objective's `fast-burn (page)` with the check as `slice` | about 4 h (two failed runs in 4 h and 30% of 12 h) |
| `people-can-play-games-end-to-end`, one game | Break one game's journey on dev only | `fast-burn (page)`, `slice` naming that journey | about 4 h |
| any Health objective, silence | Disable the Health schedule for 9 h | `No events for People can sign in, …` (page) | 8 h + 5 min after the last run |
| `the-app-serves-people` at low traffic | A dev build answering 5xx on one sync request | `1 or more bad in 1h at low traffic (push)`, `slice` `sync`; at three, `(page)` | about 2 min |
| `the-app-serves-people`, `changes-are-saved` above the floor | 5% of `/api/mutations` answering 5xx under ≥ 60 people-shaped requests an hour | `fast-burn (page)` per route group | 3 to 5 min |
| `saves-and-loads-*`, `signing-in-*`, `the-app-never-leaves-people-waiting` | Add 0.8 s, 1.2 s, then 3 s to sync or session responses | the matching `fast-burn` above the floor, or the low-traffic count below it | 2 to 5 min |
| `background-work-gets-done` | Make the dev drain throw every round | pipeline `Scheduled outbox drain keeps failing` and the objective's `fast-burn (page)` | about 4 min |
| certificates | none needed: the rule reads the probe's certificate expiry | | |
| routing | Set `paging_environment` to `dev` for one test above | IRM paging chain, important notifications | as above |

Already proven on real data: replaying the generated rules against
production on 2026-10-24, the per-slice fast burn of `the-app-serves-people`
fires for sync from 14:01 to 14:07 and session from 14:03 to 14:05 (a page
at about 14:03 and 14:05 with the 2-minute hold), and the low-traffic count
fires at 15:45 for the session failure at 15:01. The previous rules raised
nothing. The dev-only fault switches above (5xx, latency, drain) do not exist
yet; until they do, the logic is proven by the code tests
(`libs/observability-shared-ts/tests/slo-live.test.ts`: healthy stays quiet,
failures and slowness break the right objectives, a run of failed hourly
checks pages, **one broken game among thirteen pages**).

## Validation

- **OpenSLO**: every objective file and the generated policies pass `oslo
  validate` (`go install github.com/OpenSLO/oslo/cmd/oslo@latest`). The
  in-repo reader refuses what `oslo` refuses (display names over 63
  characters, label keys with `/`, more than one condition per policy), so
  `bun verify` catches it without the Go tool.
- **Generation**: `bun run libs/observability-shared-ts/scripts/slo-generate.ts`
  writes three files; `tests/slo-artifacts.test.ts` fails on drift, on output
  that differs between runs, on a `grafana_slo` declared anywhere but the
  generated `slos.tf`, on an objective without a Grafana SLO or runbook
  section, on an unsliced rule for a sliced indicator, and on a `by (…)`
  label no series carries.
- **Queries**: all 150 generated rule and budget expressions parse and
  evaluate against production and dev; people's requests, saves and latency
  return the values above; Health and Synthetic Monitoring return nothing
  until deployed and installed, and their silence rules fire now, as they
  should.
- **OpenTofu**: `tofu validate` passes for bootstrap and foundation, against
  the provider's own schema. `tofu plan` was **not run**: plans run only in
  GitHub Actions (repository rule), and Actions is out of budget.

## Residual gaps

What people could experience as broken while every objective is green, and
what to do about each.

1. **The Health workflow does not run** (Actions budget). Its silence pages
   once deployed. Restore the budget (reliability work 1).
2. **Production background work and its heartbeat are silent since
   2026-10-23** (reliability work 2).
3. **Realtime freshness is unmeasured**: how long a move takes to reach the
   other player, reconnection success, presence. The broker check proves
   people can reach it; the Health journeys prove moves arrive. Recommend a
   client metric of delivery delay per update and connect/reconnect outcome,
   exported through the browser telemetry ingress.
4. **Move durability in production** (no loss, duplication or reordering) is
   proven on the harness (Quint models) but not measured live. Recommend a
   reconciliation job comparing mutation receipts with stored rows.
5. **Client experience**: Web Vitals and JavaScript errors reach Loki as
   logs, not metrics; failed API calls seen by browsers are not counted.
   Recommend a page-load and JS-errors-per-session objective from Loki
   recording rules.
6. **Journey duration**: a slow but passing journey looks perfect (evidence
   has no timing). Record each feature's duration with its result.
7. **Sign-in stops at Google's page** in the checks; a failure after a real
   password is unseen until people report it or their session requests fail
   (the session objectives).
8. **Slices**: no region or client version label; a failure local to one
   client build hides in its route group. Add bounded labels first.
9. **One probe location** (Oregon), and Synthetic Monitoring uses about 96k
   of the free 100k runs a month: a second location needs a paid plan or
   fewer checks.
10. **Counting**: Worker isolate counters are counted as growth plus each new
    series' first value; histogram bucket series can differ by a few events
    (seen in dev). Delta temporality or native histograms would be exact.
11. **Unverified until applied**: the probe name `Oregon` (current in
    Grafana's docs), the Grafana SLO dashboard URL (`/d/grafana_slo_app-<id>`)
    and whether a check fails on a 426 body regex as documented. The silence
    and fast-burn rules fire if any is wrong.
12. **Tempo span metrics** are off; not needed by the current objectives.
